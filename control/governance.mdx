Safety is engineering. Governance is who is allowed to decide, and how you prove afterwards that they did.

The rule that keeps this simple: **no birth grants itself anything.** Every permission, every level, every widening is a human action with a name attached.

## Who may do what

| Action | Who may do it |
| --- | --- |
| Birth an agent at L1–L2, R0/R1 tools | Builder |
| Raise autonomy to L3–L4 | Owner of the business process |
| Any L5, or any R3 tool | Platform owner, with a written scope |
| Change a contract's scope | The person accountable for the affected system |
| Use the kill switch | Anyone in the on-call rotation, unilaterally |
| Override a refusal or a gate | Nobody: the gate is code, not policy |

The asymmetry between "raise autonomy" and "stop everything" is deliberate. Granting capability should be deliberative and slightly annoying. Withdrawing it must be trivial and instant.

## The review gate

Before a birth touches real work:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Remit review">
    Someone who did not write it restates the remit in one paragraph. If they cannot, it is not defined well enough to evaluate.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Contract review">
    Autonomy, budget, scope, tools, done-condition, escalation. Reviewed as a security artefact, not a config file.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Evaluation review">
    Calibration and coverage inside bounds, adversarial cases reviewed, held-out slice passed. See [Evaluation](/build/evaluation).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Recorded decision">
    Who approved, at what level, with what scope, on what evidence. One row, permanently.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Data

- **Tenant isolation** is structural. Memory, traces and credentials are scoped per tenant; cross-tenant retrieval is not a setting because it should not be possible.
- **Retention is declared.** Episodic detail, semantic facts and traces have different lifetimes, and every birth states them.
- **Deletion is answerable.** A request to remove an entity is executable across all three memory stores and the trace index, because a self-hosted system must be able to honour it.
- **Personal data is minimised.** A forecaster rarely needs to know who a person is to predict what a system will do. Where identity is not the signal, it is not stored.

## Transparency

When Omenra acts on someone's behalf, that person can find out:

- that a birth was involved,
- what prediction drove the action,
- what it was permitted to do,
- who was accountable for the contract.

An automated decision that cannot be attributed to a contract and a person is not permitted to affect anyone outside the organisation.

## Incidents

An incident is any case where a birth acted outside its contract, caused harm, or produced a consequence a human had to undo. Incidents are:

1. **stopped** first, investigated second,
2. **written up** within the week, including the trace,
3. **encoded as an evaluation case** so the same failure cannot return quietly,
4. **published internally**, because a near-miss nobody hears about is a near-miss that happens twice.
