This is a direction, not a schedule. It is ordered by dependency, because most of what looks like a choice here is actually a prerequisite.

## Now: the vocabulary and the first birth

- **Contract model.** Autonomy levels, budgets, scope, done-conditions and escalation, written down and enforced at dispatch.
- **Agent runtime.** The observe → predict → act loop with memory, tool dispatch and a trace that reconstructs decisions.
- **First forecaster.** One narrow, high-frequency domain where calibration can be measured honestly within weeks.
- **Evaluation harness.** Reliability curves, coverage, abstention scoring, held-out slices.

The first forecaster matters more than it looks. Everything else in this company is a claim until a calibration curve exists and is real.

## Next: the body

- **Tool registry** with risk classes and reversibility, so permissions follow actions rather than agents.
- **Bots** in real channels: triage, intake, status. Read-only first, always.
- **Swarm orchestration** with an arbiter, budget at the arbiter, and cancellation that propagates.
- **Second and third forecasters** in different horizons. The interesting question is whether one proficiency transfers.

## Later: the hard parts

- **Embodiment.** Simulation-first, supervised, with the safety envelope in the control loop. Deliberately last: a physical mistake is the only kind that cannot be rolled back.
- **Self-hosted deployment** for partners whose data cannot leave their network.
- **Multi-tenant platform** with structural isolation, for organisations running many births at once.
- **Long-horizon forecasting.** The most ambitious and least evidenced. It ships when it calibrates, not when it demos.

## What would change the order

A roadmap that cannot be reordered is a fantasy. Three things would move it:

| If this happens | Then |
| --- | --- |
| General models get cheap and good at tool use faster than expected | Move to orchestration and evaluation sooner; build fewer specialist models |
| Calibration proves harder to hold in production than in evaluation | Everything stops until drift detection is solved |
| A design partner brings a problem that needs embodiment first | Embodiment moves up, with the safety envelope shipped ahead of it |

## What we deliberately do not ship

- A general-purpose assistant. The market is well served, and "chat with your data" is not a prediction.
- An autonomous agent with no contract surface, however impressive the demo.
- Anything in the [refusal list](/control/safety#what-we-will-not-build).

<Note>
  Dates are omitted on purpose. A roadmap with a calendar invites the question "is it late?" instead of "is it right?". For a platform defining its own vocabulary, right matters more.
</Note>
